## Points to keep in mind

- **Comparisons are a different kind of data.** Every comparison statement has exactly 5 words and is about numbers only, while the other types cover six knowledge topics. A difference between comparison and the other types can come from topic or length, not only from sentence structure.
- **Conjunctions and disjunctions are about three times longer** than affirmative and negated statements.
- **Topic sizes are uneven.** `cities` makes up about half of the affirmative and negated statements. Sample per topic if the types should be compared on the same topic mix.
- **Affirmative and negated rows are aligned.** Row *i* of `neg_<topic>.csv` negates row *i* of `<topic>.csv`, so the pairs share a `group` key.
- **Use `group` for train/test splits** (e.g. scikit-learn `GroupKFold`), so the same city, number pair or fact never appears on both sides.
- **Conjunctions and disjunctions reuse atomic facts** from the affirmative sets. That does not matter for within-type probing, but it does for cross-type transfer.
